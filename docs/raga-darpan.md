# Rāg darpaṇ

A raga made for PHI by the owner (ΦNYX) and Claude on 26 October 2026. *Darpaṇ* means
mirror. The raga takes its name, its shape and its feeling from Faqīrullāh's *Rāg Darpaṇ*
(1666). Its sheet is the last in `Source/core/PhiRagas.cpp`, and `testRagaSheets` checks that it
is its own mirror.

## The treatise behind it

- **The book:** Saif Khān "Faqīrullāh", a noble at Aurangzeb's court, finished the *Rāg Darpaṇ*
  in 1666. Much of it carries Raja Man Singh Tomar's *Mānakutūhala* (Gwalior, around 1500) into
  Persian, and the rest describes the music and musicians of his own time.
- **Ras:** Faqīrullāh wrote as a *rasika*, a listener who tastes ras, more than as a theorist. He
  names the ras of several ragas and defines ras as "inflaming the passion and pleasing the
  heart through listening".
- **Music's essence:** he calls it *lazzat*: to stir tender sympathy in the heart, above all
  *'ishq*, love.
- **Dhrupad:** it can carry all nine ras, but its subject is chiefly love and what love does to
  the heart.
- **Nāyikā-bheda:** between his chapters on instruments and on voices he set a Persian
  catalogue of heroines, so listeners could know dhrupad's moods.
- **Ta'thīr (effect):** music works by sound alone, without words or pictures.

These points come from scholars' accounts of Shahab Sarmadee's edition and translation,
*Tarjuma-i-Mānakutūhala & Risāla-i-Rāgadarpaṇa* (1996), not from the Persian text itself.

## The mirror

- **Svaras:** S r G M d N, in pūrvī thāṭ, without Pa. Reflected about Sa (and so about Ma), r
  and N trade places, as do G and d, while Sa and tīvra Ma stay put. The raga is its own
  reflection.
- **Two halves:** the lower half S r G M climbs in steps of ½, 1½ and 1. The upper half
  M d N Ṡ climbs in steps of 1, 1½ and ½, the same steps in reverse. Each half holds the
  augmented second of the Persian ḥijāz, in memory of the treatise carrying Indian music into
  Persian.
- **Tuning:** tīvra Ma is exactly the middle of the octave (600 cents, the square root of 2),
  the mirror's plane. The rest are just: r 16/15, G 5/4, d 8/5, N 15/8. Each pair adds up to an
  octave: 16/15 × 15/8 and 5/4 × 8/5 are both 2.

## Movement

- **Ārohī:** S r G M, G M d N Ṡ. Every ascent rests on Ma before it crosses to the upper half.
- **Avarohī:** Ṡ N d M, d M G r S. This is the ārohī reflected, svara for svara.
- **The rule:** any phrase may be answered by its reflection. A phrase in the lower half is
  answered in the upper, and the other way round.
- **Vādī Ma, samvādī Sa:** the only two svaras that are their own reflection.
- **Pakaḍ:** G M d M G | r G M d N d M G r. Both phrases are palindromes about Ma.

## Ornaments

- **Gamak on Ma** (3 Hz), the slow dhrupad shake of Gwalior, Man Singh's city.
- **Āndolan on komal dha** (15 cents at 0.4 Hz): the longing svara, reaching up toward Ṡa.
- **Kaṇ from Ṡa onto ni:** a glance that touches the reflection and pulls back.
- **Mīṇḍ from ga through Ma to dha:** crossing the mirror. On a track, play ga and then dha
  legato.

## Ras and bhāv

- **Ras:** śṛṅgāra in separation (*vipralambha*): tender longing, the *'ishq* Faqīrullāh hears at
  the heart of music.
- **Nāyikā:** the *vāsakasajjā*, who dresses at her mirror at dusk, waiting for her beloved, as
  ragamala paintings often show her. This is our choice, not something the treatise says of
  this raga.
- **Time:** dusk, the boundary where day meets its reflection, night.
