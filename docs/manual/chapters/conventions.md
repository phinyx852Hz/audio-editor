# About This Manual

This manual describes every part of PHI, from opening your first file to mastering an album, building a mix, playing instruments, placing sound in a sphere and sending a performance live. You can read it from the start, or go straight to the chapter you need and follow the links from there.

## How the manual is laid out

The chapters are grouped in seven parts.

- **Getting Started** installs PHI, walks around its window and explains how it is driven from the keyboard. Read [Working from the Keyboard](#keyboard) even if you only read one chapter: everything else assumes it.
- **Editing** covers the wave editor: files, recording, selection and editing, markers, processing, the spectral view, repair and restoration, tempo and warping, and Nyquist effects and macros.
- **Mastering** covers the Master Section and the Device Chain, the effects that come with PHI, the meters, reference tracks and the Master Assistant, exporting, batch processing, albums and metadata.
- **Producing and Performing** covers the Find panel, the mixer and patchbay, the MIDI editor, the clip launcher, the samplers, the instruments, the trackers, surround and ambisonics, the Visualizer, controllers and MIDI mapping, and video and streaming.
- **Composing** covers the Compose, Mode and Research menus: writing scores by the methods of many traditions, and the modes, harmonies, tunings and rhythms they use.
- **Φ** is a chapter of its own, about the golden ratio in PHI: what it is, where PHI uses it, and why.
- **Making PHI Yours** covers themes, colours and settings.

The appendices list every default keyboard shortcut, every file and folder PHI uses, and the people whose work PHI is built on. The index at the end lists every feature, menu and term, with links to each place it is described.

## Conventions

**Menus** are written with a `>` between levels: *File > Export* means the Export item in the File menu.

**Keys** are drawn as keycaps: `Ctrl+Shift+E` means hold Ctrl and Shift and press E. Two keys separated by a slash, such as `Ctrl+W / Super+W`, are alternatives. Every shortcut can be changed (see [Changing shortcuts](#change-shortcuts)), and this manual always gives the defaults.

**Mouse** actions are written plainly: click, double-click, right-click, drag. *Wheel* means the mouse wheel or a two-finger scroll.

**Paths** such as `~/.config/phi` are folders in your home directory. [Files and Folders](#files-folders) lists them all.

**Inspirations.** PHI often says which program a feature is modelled on ("like WaveLab's Master Section"). This is to help you if you already know that program. It does not mean PHI shares any code or artwork with it.

Call-out boxes mark things worth knowing:

:::

::: tip

:::
