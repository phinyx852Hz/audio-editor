# Modes / Africa

## Ethiopia

Ethiopian music is built on four pentatonic modes (qenyet): tezeta, bati, ambassel and anchihoye,
each with major and minor forms, sung and played on the krar, masenqo and washint. The Ethiopian
Orthodox Church has its own chant, zema, in three modes credited to Saint Yared.

Yared (6th century) is said to have composed the church's hymns and its notation. The secular
modes were named and theorized in the twentieth century; the Ethiopian jazz and pop of the 1960s
and 70s, heard in the *Éthiopiques* series, are built on them.

- [Music of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ethiopia) and
  [Qenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qenet), Wikipedia.
- Kay Kaufman Shelemay, *Music, Ritual, and Falasha History* (Michigan State University Press, 1986).

## Zimbabwe (Shona)

The Shona mbira dzavadzimu, "mbira of the ancestors", has 22 to 28 metal keys on a wooden board,
played in a gourd resonator. Each instrument has its own heptatonic tuning (no two are alike), and
pieces such as *Nhemamusasa* are cycles of interlocking parts, played at the bira ceremony to
call the ancestors.

Mbira have been played in the region for over a thousand years. Missionaries and the colonial
government discouraged them; the music revived with independence (1980), and Paul Berliner's *The
Soul of Mbira* (1978) and Thomas Mapfumo's chimurenga music took it worldwide.

- [Mbira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbira), Wikipedia.
- Paul F. Berliner, *The Soul of Mbira* (University of California Press, 1978).
- [Mbira.org](https://www.mbira.org).

## Korekore and Sena (matepe)

The matepe (or hera) is the mbira of the Korekore of northern Zimbabwe and the Sena of
Mozambique: 22 to 28 keys in a narrow, partly hexatonic tuning, played in cycles of interlocking
patterns like the Shona mbira, often in spirit-possession ceremonies.

Andrew Tracey documented its tunings and playing in *African Music* (1970). The International
Library of African Music, founded by his father Hugh Tracey, holds its recordings.

- [International Library of African Music](https://www.ru.ac.za/ilam/), Rhodes University.
- Andrew Tracey, "The Matepe Mbira Music of Rhodesia", *African Music* 4/4 (1970).
