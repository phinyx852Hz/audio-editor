# Acoustic Ecology / Composers and projects

## Forerunners

Acoustic ecology drew on a half-century of composers who let the sounds of the world into
music. Luigi Russolo's manifesto *The Art of Noises* (1913) called for a music of the city's
noises and built intonarumori to make them. Pierre Schaeffer's musique concrète (from 1948)
composed with recorded sounds, and his idea of reduced listening, hearing a sound for itself
apart from its source, is the opposite pole to Schafer's, who wanted sounds heard in their
place. John Cage's *4′33″* (1952) framed the sounds of the room as the piece (the next
entry).

Luc Ferrari's *Presque rien no. 1, ou Le lever du jour au bord de la mer* (1967–70), a
morning on a beach in Yugoslavia compressed into twenty minutes with almost no
intervention, is the first great soundscape composition. Max Neuhaus's *Listen* (1966) took
audiences on walks with LISTEN stamped on their hands, and his *Times Square* (1977) hid a
drone beneath a subway grating. Annea Lockwood and Pauline Oliveros began in the same years.

## John Cage

John Cage (1912–1992), an American composer, visual artist and music theorist, opened music
to the sounds of the environment more than anyone before Schafer. A student of Henry Cowell
and Arnold Schoenberg, he drew his main influences from Indian philosophy and Zen Buddhism,
which he studied in the late 1940s (attending D. T. Suzuki's lectures), and from 1951
composed by chance with the *I Ching*. In "Experimental Music" (1957) he called music "a
purposeless play", "simply a way of waking up to the very life we're living".

His *4′33″* (1952), premiered by David Tudor at Woodstock, New York, on 29 August 1952, asks
the performer not to play for its whole length: its content is the sound of the environment
the audience hears. For acoustic ecology it is the founding gesture, the frame of music
opened to the whole soundscape. Around it stand *Imaginary Landscape No. 4* (1951) for twelve
radios, *Williams Mix* (1952–53) for eight magnetic tapes, *Child of Tree* (1975) and
*Branches* (1976), played on plants such as cactus, *Inlets* (1977) on water-filled conch
shells, and *Roaratorio* (1979), which sets his text on *Finnegans Wake* among field
recordings made around Ireland. He was also an amateur mycologist and a co-founder of the New
York Mycological Society. His lectures are collected in *Silence* (1961); he received the
Kyoto Prize in 1989.

## Soundscape composition

Soundscape composition is music made from environmental recordings in which the place stays
recognisable: the listener is meant to know where the sounds come from and to hear the place
anew, not to forget it, as musique concrète asked. Barry Truax gave its principles: the
listener's recognition of the source is kept, the listener's knowledge of the place is
invoked, the composer's knowledge of it shapes the work, and the work changes the listener's
understanding of the world.

It grew at Simon Fraser University from the World Soundscape Project, whose radio series
*Soundscapes of Canada* (1974) included the first such pieces, and spread with the composers
of the Vancouver school (Truax, Westerkamp, Claude Schryer, Darren Copeland) and the
Canadian and European radio art of the 1980s and 1990s. Its forms run from the barely edited
"phonography" of a single place to works where recordings are transformed by granular
synthesis, filtering and spatialisation.

## Barry Truax

Barry Truax (born 1947) is a Canadian composer and the theorist of acoustic communication.
He joined the World Soundscape Project in 1973, edited its *Handbook for Acoustic Ecology*
(1978), and taught at Simon Fraser University. *Acoustic Communication* (1984) recasts
acoustic ecology as the study of how sound mediates between listener and environment, a
model of information exchange rather than of signal transfer.

He was the first to make granular synthesis run in real time: his PODX system made
*Riverrun* (1986), a river built from millions of tiny grains, and he extended it to
granulate recorded sound, which joined his synthesis to his soundscape work in pieces such as
*Pacific* (1990) and *Basilica* (1992), the latter from the bells of a church in Quebec City.

  [Granular synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_synthesis), Wikipedia.
- [Barry Truax](https://www.sfu.ca/~truax/), his pages at Simon Fraser University.
- Barry Truax, *Acoustic Communication* (Ablex, 1984; 2nd ed. 2001).

## Hildegard Westerkamp

Hildegard Westerkamp (born 1946) is a German-Canadian composer, radio artist and teacher, a
member of the World Soundscape Project from the early 1970s and a founding member of the
World Forum for Acoustic Ecology. Her radio programme *Soundwalking* on Vancouver Co-op Radio
(from 1978) and her essay "Soundwalking" (1974) made the soundwalk a practice and an art form.

Her compositions put the recordist's own listening into the piece, often with her voice
speaking to the listener: *A Walk Through the City* (1981), *Cricket Voice* (1987), made in
the Zone of Silence in the Mexican desert, *Kits Beach Soundwalk* (1989), in which she
filters away the city's roar from a Vancouver beach to reveal the tiny sounds of barnacles,
*Beneath the Forest Floor* (1992) from the old-growth forests of British Columbia, and
*Gently Penetrating Beneath the Sounding Surfaces of Another Place* (1997) from India.

- [Hildegard Westerkamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_Westerkamp), Wikipedia.
- [Hildegard Westerkamp](https://www.hildegardwesterkamp.ca), her own site, with her writings.
- Andra McCartney, *Sounding Places: Situated Conversations through the Soundscape Compositions
  of Hildegard Westerkamp* (PhD thesis, York University, 1999).

## Soundwalk

A soundwalk is a walk made for listening, alone or in a group and often in silence, through a
place whose sounds are attended to as a composition; the term and practice come from the World
Soundscape Project and Westerkamp. A soundwalk can be led live, scored as a set of
instructions, or recorded and composed as a piece. Its descendants include audio walks
such as Janet Cardiff's (from 1991) and the listening walks of sound-art festivals and urban
planners.

- [Soundwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundwalk) and
  [Janet Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Cardiff), Wikipedia.
- Hildegard Westerkamp, "Soundwalking", *Sound Heritage* 3/4 (1974).

## Pauline Oliveros and Deep Listening

Pauline Oliveros (1932–2016), an American composer and accordionist born in Houston, Texas,
was a central figure of post-war experimental and electronic music. With Ramón Sender and
Morton Subotnick she was one of the original members of the San Francisco Tape Music Center in
the early 1960s, and its first director when it moved to Mills College as the Center for
Contemporary Music. She taught at Mills, the University of California, San Diego (1967–81),
Oberlin and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Her electronic *I of IV* (1966) was made in Hugh
Le Caine's studio in Toronto, and *A Little Noise in the System* (1967) is among the earliest
contemporary noise works. She often improvised with the Expanded Instrument System, an
electronic signal-processing system of her own design, and from 1987 played an accordion
retuned to just intonation.

In 1988 she descended fourteen feet into the Dan Harpole Cistern at Port Townsend,
Washington, to record, and coined "deep listening", a pun that grew into "an aesthetic ...
designed to inspire both trained and untrained performers to practice the art of listening and
responding to environmental conditions in solo and ensemble situations". With Stuart Dempster
and Panaiotis she formed the Deep Listening Band (later with David Gamper), which plays in
caves, cathedrals and cisterns. Deep Listening became a programme of her foundation (1985),
renamed the Deep Listening Institute in 2005, with retreats and certification; the Center for
Deep Listening at Rensselaer has been its steward since 2015.

Its technique is sonic awareness, as the musicologist Heidi Von Gunden named Oliveros's
theory: "the ability to consciously focus attention upon environmental and musical sound",
needing "continual alertness and an inclination to be always listening". It works between two
modes, focal attention and global attention (awareness), which Oliveros drew as a dot and a
circle in scores such as *Rose Moon* (1977); later she quartered the circle into actively
making sound, imagining sound, listening to present sound and remembering past sound, the model
of the *Sonic Meditations*. Those are text scores, prose instructions for anyone ("Teach
Yourself to Fly" is the first), collected with her later ones in the *Anthology of Text Scores
1971–2013*. The best known is *The Tuning Meditation*, first performed in 1981 by seventy-five singers
directed by William Duckworth along the spiral walkway of the Guggenheim Museum, New York, for
Elaine Summers's *Crows Nest*, and open to anyone regardless of training. Each singer listens
inwardly for a tone and sings it on one breath; listens to the whole field; tunes as exactly as
possible to a distant voice; listens again; sings a new tone no one else is singing; and goes
on alternating between tuning to another and offering a tone of their own, one tone to a
breath, until everyone ends together ("It happens"). IONE, Oliveros's partner, led a *World
Wide Tuning Meditation* online in April 2020.
Practised, sonic awareness makes, in Von Gunden's words, "complex sound masses
possessing a strong tonal center".

## Annea Lockwood

Annea Lockwood (born 1939), a New Zealand-born American composer, made rivers her subject.
From 1966 she gathered the *River Archive* of recordings of rivers around the world; *A Sound
Map of the Hudson River* (1982) follows the river from its source in the Adirondacks to the
Atlantic, and *A Sound Map of the Danube* (2005) and *A Sound Map of the Housatonic River*
(2010) continued the series, with the voices of people who live beside them. Her earlier
*Piano Transplants* (1969–72) burned, drowned and planted pianos in the landscape.

- [Annea Lockwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annea_Lockwood), Wikipedia.

## John Luther Adams

John Luther Adams (born 1953), an American composer who lived for decades in Alaska, writes
music that seeks to be a place rather than describe it, an ecological music in Schafer's
spirit. *The Place Where You Go to Listen* (2006), at the Museum of the North in Fairbanks,
turns live data from earthquakes, the sun, the moon and the aurora into continuous sound;
*Inuksuit* (2009) is for up to 99 percussionists spread through a landscape; *Become Ocean*
(2013) won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2014. His books are *Winter Music* (2004) and *The
Place Where You Go to Listen* (2009).

- [John Luther Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Luther_Adams), Wikipedia.
- John Luther Adams, *The Place Where You Go to Listen: In Search of an Ecology of Music*
  (Wesleyan University Press, 2009).

## David Dunn

David Dunn (born 1953), an American composer and sound artist, works between music and
science: *Chaos and the Emergent Mind of the Pond* (1991) from the underwater insects of
ponds, *Angels and Insects* (1999), and *The Sound of Light in Trees* (2006), recordings of
bark beetles inside piñon pines that he made with the scientist Richard Hofstetter, whose
studies followed the beetle plague of the American West. His book *Why Do Whales and Children
Sing?* (1999) is a guide to listening to nature.

- [David Dunn (composer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dunn_(composer)), Wikipedia.

## Field recordists

Recording the world for its own sake, phonography, is acoustic ecology's widest practice.
Among its artists: Chris Watson (born 1952), once of Cabaret Voltaire and a sound recordist
for the BBC's natural history films, whose *Weather Report* (2003) compresses a day on a
Scottish loch and a glacier in Iceland; Francisco López, whose *La Selva* (1998) is an
unaltered Costa Rican rainforest offered as a sound matter rather than a portrait; Jana
Winderen, who records under the sea and ice with hydrophones; and Leah Barclay, whose River
Listening projects in Australia join art to the monitoring of rivers.

- [Field recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_recording),

  2013).

## Sound maps

A sound map places recordings on a map so that a listener can hear a city or a country place
by place, a form the World Soundscape Project began with its sound maps of Vancouver and that
the web made collective. Udo Noll's radio aporee ::: maps (from 2006) gathers hundreds of
thousands of recordings worldwide; the London Sound Survey (Ian Rawes, from 2009) documented
the city and its history of sound; Cities and Memory (Stuart Fowkes, from 2014) pairs each
field recording with a reimagined composition of it; and the British Library's UK Soundmap
(2010–11) collected recordings from the public for its sound archive.

- [Sound map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_map), Wikipedia.
- [radio aporee ::: maps](https://aporee.org/maps/) and
  [Cities and Memory](https://citiesandmemory.com).

## The World Forum for Acoustic Ecology

The World Forum for Acoustic Ecology (WFAE), founded in 1993 at an international conference,
The Tuning of the World, in Banff, Alberta, is an international association of affiliated
organisations and individuals who share a concern for the state of the world's soundscapes.
Its affiliates are national and regional societies (Canada, the United States, the United
Kingdom and Ireland, Japan, Australia and New Zealand, Finland, Greece, Germany and others).
Its mission is education in listening, research into the social, cultural, scientific and
ecological sides of the sonic environment, publishing, the protection of natural soundscapes
and of times and places of quiet, and the design of acoustically balanced environments.

Its journal *Soundscape: The Journal of Acoustic Ecology* has led the field since the early
2000s; its successor, the online *Acoustic Ecology Review*, is published with Simon Fraser
University. The WFAE Library is an open-access, community-built catalogue of articles,
publications, audiovisual and creative works, projects, organisations, radio, podcasts and
sound maps in acoustic ecology, soundscape studies and ecoacoustics.
