# Sampling

## The samplers compared

The instruments below are the landmarks: each made a technique possible, or cheap enough to
become common.

| Instrument | Maker | Year | What it brought |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chamberlin | Harry Chamberlin | late 1940s to 1950s | Tape recordings of real instruments under each key |
| Mellotron | Streetly Electronics | 1963 | The Chamberlin's idea made in Britain; the sound of 1960s rock |
| Fairlight CMI | Fairlight | 1979 | The first commercial digital sampler, with screen, light pen and sequencer |
| Synclavier | New England Digital | early 1980s (sampling) | Sampling and hard-disk recording in a studio system |
| Linn LM-1 | Linn Electronics | 1980 | The first drum machine with digitally recorded drums |
| Emulator | E-mu Systems | 1981 | A sampling keyboard without a computer; the Emulator II (1984) everywhere |
| Kurzweil K250 | Kurzweil | 1984 | Realistic multisampled piano and orchestra |
| Mirage | Ensoniq | 1984 | Sampling for under 1,700 dollars |
| SK-1 | Casio | 1985 | A toy sampler, loved for its lo-fi sound |
| Ultimate Soundtracker | Karsten Obarski (Amiga) | 1987 | Sample-based songs on a home computer, the MOD format |
| S900, S1000 | Akai | 1986, 1988 | The studio rack sampler; S1000's 16 bits and its disk format |
| SP-1200 | E-mu Systems | 1987 | 12-bit, 26 kHz drums and chops: the sound of golden-age hip hop |
| MPC60 | Akai with Roger Linn | 1988 | Velocity pads with sampling and sequencing; the producer's instrument |
| ReCycle | Propellerhead | 1994 | Loops cut into slices that follow any tempo |
| GigaSampler | NemeSys | 1998 | Samples streamed from disk; deep libraries |
| Kontakt | Native Instruments | 2002 | The scripted library format most commercial instruments use |
| Simpler, Drum Rack | Ableton | 2005, 2007 | Drag a clip onto an instrument; pads that hold whole effect chains |

In PHI, Simplest follows Live's Simpler (one sound, Classic and 1-Shot modes), the Slicer an
MPC-style pad sampler after Ninjas2, Cubase's Drum Machine and Live's Drum Rack, and the Tracker
the Amiga's trackers.

- [Sampler (musical instrument)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampler_(musical_instrument)), Wikipedia.
- [Mellotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellotron), [Fairlight CMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairlight_CMI),
  [E-mu Emulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_Emulator), [E-mu SP-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mu_SP-1200)
  and [Akai MPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akai_MPC), Wikipedia.

## Records that shaped sampling

Each of these made a sampling technique famous; many later records learned from them.

| Record | Artist | Year | Technique |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Étude aux chemins de fer* | Pierre Schaeffer | 1948 | Musique concrète from recorded trains |
| *Doctor Who* theme | Delia Derbyshire (Ron Grainer) | 1963 | Tape loops and edited tape |
| "Tomorrow Never Knows" | The Beatles | 1966 | Tape loops played live |
| "Strawberry Fields Forever" | The Beatles | 1967 | Mellotron flutes |
| *Never for Ever* | Kate Bush | 1980 | Among the first albums with the Fairlight |
| *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts* | Brian Eno and David Byrne | 1981 | Found voices over grooves |
| "Planet Rock" | Afrika Bambaataa | 1982 | The Fairlight orchestra hit, Kraftwerk interpolated |
| "Owner of a Lonely Heart" | Yes | 1983 | Orchestra hits and stabs (Trevor Horn) |
| "Beat Box" | Art of Noise | 1983 | Fairlight collage of drums and noises |
| "Pump Up the Volume" | M\|A\|R\|R\|S | 1987 | A dance hit made of dozens of samples |
| *Paid in Full* | Eric B. & Rakim | 1987 | Looped funk and soul breaks |
| *It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back* | Public Enemy | 1988 | Dense layers of samples (the Bomb Squad) |
| *3 Feet High and Rising* | De La Soul | 1989 | Playful collage; an early sampling lawsuit |
| *Paul's Boutique* | Beastie Boys | 1989 | Collage from hundreds of samples (the Dust Brothers) |
| Jungle and drum and bass | many | early 1990s | The Amen break chopped, sped up and stretched |
| *Endtroducing.....* | DJ Shadow | 1996 | An album built almost entirely from samples on the MPC60 |
| *Play* | Moby | 1999 | Field recordings and old blues sampled into pop |
| *Since I Left You* | The Avalanches | 2000 | Thousands of samples, sewn together |
| *The College Dropout* | Kanye West | 2004 | Sped-up soul ("chipmunk soul") |
| *Donuts* | J Dilla | 2006 | Chops played loose on the MPC, off the grid |

## Further reading

- Mark Katz, *Capturing Sound: How Technology Has Changed Music* (University of California
  Press, 2004; revised 2010), with a chapter on sampling.
- Joseph G. Schloss, *Making Beats: The Art of Sample-Based Hip-Hop* (Wesleyan University Press,
  2004; 2nd ed. 2014).
- Curtis Roads, *Microsound* (MIT Press, 2001), on granular techniques.
- Kembrew McLeod and Peter DiCola, *Creative License: The Law and Culture of Digital Sampling*
  (Duke University Press, 2011).
- [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/), a database of who sampled what.
